Areas of work

Psychotherapy for children

Children often express their inner world through play, drawings and stories rather than words.

Play therapy. In work with children, play is approached as the main language through which a child can tell what they are going through. Through toys, drawings and stories the child has the opportunity to express their experiences, worries and wishes; the therapist listens closely to this play and makes sense of it together with the child.

Parent counselling. The process is supported by regular meetings with the parents. These meetings explore the meanings behind the child’s behaviour and the relationships within the family.

The first meeting is usually with the parents, where the child’s development and the reason for seeking help are discussed. Sessions with children are held in person; meetings with parents can be in person or online.

When do parents seek help?

Children rarely show their difficulties in words; they appear in a change in behaviour, in sleep, eating, play or at school. Sometimes intense outbursts of anger or withdrawal come to the fore; sometimes fears, difficulty separating or problems with friends. Changes such as the birth of a sibling, parents’ separation, a move, a loss or a difficult experience can also affect a child deeply. There does not always need to be a clear problem: parents may also come to follow their child’s development or to think together about ways of building a healthier relationship with their child.

Whatever the reason, the work listens to the child rather than to the symptom alone: what their experience means to them, and how they tell it in their play and their words.

How does the process work?

The first meeting is usually with the parents alone. In it, the child’s development, the family’s history and the reason for seeking help are discussed. Then there are introductory meetings with the child, and we agree together how to work.

In sessions, the child expresses themselves through toys, drawings and stories. The frequency of sessions is arranged according to the child’s situation and needs; sessions last about 45 minutes on average. Throughout the process there are regular meetings with the parents, both to accompany the child’s process and to make room for the parents’ own questions.

Frequently asked questions

How should I tell my child they will be going to therapy?

A simple and honest explanation is enough; for example, you can say they will be meeting someone with whom they can play, talk and think together about their feelings and experiences. We can also think together in the first meeting about how to tell them.

Can I find out what is discussed in sessions?

What the child shares in sessions is not passed on in detail, to protect their trust and privacy. General reflections on the process are discussed in parent meetings.

What age range do you work with?

I generally work with children aged 3–12. For older children, please see the Adolescents page.

Can sessions with children be held online?

Sessions with children are held in person. Meetings with parents can be in person or online.
