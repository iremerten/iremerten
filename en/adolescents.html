Areas of work

Psychotherapy for adolescents

Adolescence is an intense period in which the body, identity and relationships with the family are reshaped.

Work with adolescents takes place within a frame that respects the young person’s own words and privacy. Sessions unfold as a space where they can express themselves and be listened to without judgement.

When needed, separate meetings with parents can be arranged, always with the young person’s knowledge.

When do people seek help?

Difficulty in adolescence has no single form and does not fit into a list. Sometimes struggles in friendships or first relationships come to the fore; sometimes questions about who one is and the search for identity. Difficult experiences within the family, losses and traumatic experiences, problems at school or self-harming behaviour may also be reasons for seeking help. And sometimes the young person simply says, “I want to talk to someone.”

Whatever the reason, the sessions listen to the young person rather than to the symptom alone: what their experience means to them and where it sits within their own story.

How does the process work?

The first meetings are for understanding the reason for seeking help and agreeing how we will work together. The young person and their parents usually attend these together; sometimes parents are also seen separately.

After that, sessions continue individually with the young person, once or twice a week, for about 45–50 minutes. In the psychoanalytic approach, ready-made answers are not offered. Instead, a space is opened for the young person to find their own words and to speak about their experience in their own language.

Confidentiality

What is said in sessions with an adolescent is confidential and is not shared with parents. This is the foundation of the young person’s trust in therapy. The only exception is a situation that puts the young person’s or someone else’s life at risk; in that case, the parents are informed, together with the young person wherever possible.

Frequently asked questions

My child doesn’t want to come to therapy. What can I do?

This is common. Sometimes the first step is a meeting with the parents. It is also possible for the young person to come once, see what it is like, and then decide.

Can I find out what is discussed in sessions?

The content of sessions is not shared. When it seems necessary, however, meetings with parents about the process are held with the young person’s knowledge.

Can sessions be held online?

Yes. Sessions are held in person or online.

What age range do you work with?

I generally work with young people aged 12–18. For younger children, please see the Children page.
